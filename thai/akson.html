Thai Alphabet อักษรไทย

The Thai alphabet has 44 consonants (two of which are obsolete). Each belongs to one of three classes (high, mid or low), which together with the tone marks decides the tone of a syllable. Three of the consonants, อ, ว and ย, also double as parts of vowels. Thai's 21 vowel symbols, written before, after, above or below the consonant, combine into 33 vowel spellings (24 vowels in short and long pairs, some spelt differently when a final consonant follows) and 14 special combinations (traditionally counted as 32 vowels). Thai also has its own numerals and some other special symbols.

Tones วรรณยุกต์

Thai has five tones. Which one a syllable takes depends on the class of its first consonant, whether the final consonant is live or dead, and whether it carries a tone mark. Note that the tone names "high", "mid" and "low" are unrelated to the high, mid and low consonant classes.

Tone Marks

A tone mark is written when a word’s actual tone differs from the one its spelling would otherwise give; the mark changes the tone to the one that is actually spoken.

Tone Rules

A live syllable ends in a long vowel or in -m, -n, -ng, -y or -w; a dead syllable ends in a short vowel or in -k, -t or -p. High and low class consonants take only the first two marks.
